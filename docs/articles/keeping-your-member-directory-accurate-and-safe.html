Keeping Your Member Directory Accurate and Safe

November 18, 2026

A church directory is an act of trust. When people share their address, phone number or their children's names, they're trusting the church to use that information carefully. Keeping the directory accurate and safe is part of caring for your people well.

Here are the basics for a small or mid-size church.

Collect only what you need

It's tempting to ask for everything, just in case. Resist that. The less sensitive information you hold, the less there is to protect, and the less it matters if something goes wrong.

For most churches, the essentials are:

  • Name and household
  • Phone number and email
  • Mailing address
  • Birthdays or anniversaries, if your church celebrates them
  • Children's names and ages, for ministry and check-in

Be thoughtful about anything more personal, such as health information, family circumstances or pastoral notes. If you do keep that kind of information, keep it separate and restricted to the people who need it.

Ask permission before sharing

There is a difference between the information the church office holds and what appears in a directory the whole congregation can see. Before you publish someone's details, ask them:

  • May we include you in the member directory?
  • Which details would you like listed (phone, email, address, photo)?
  • May we include your children's names?

Respect the answers. Some people have good reasons, including safety concerns, for keeping their address private. Make it easy to say no without having to explain why.

Decide who sees what

Not everyone needs access to everything. Think in layers:

  • Church office and pastor: full contact records and any pastoral or care notes
  • Ministry leaders: contact details for the people in their group or team
  • Check-in volunteers: children's names, guardians and any allergy or pickup notes, during check-in only
  • Members: only the details people agreed to share in the directory
  • Visitors and the public: nothing

Giving records deserve particular care. Generally only the people responsible for financial records should see who gave what.

Protect the information

A few simple habits go a long way:

  • Use strong, unique passwords, and don't share logins between people.
  • Remove access promptly when someone leaves a role or the staff.
  • Avoid emailing full member lists as attachments, and don't leave printed copies lying around the office.
  • Be careful with printed directories: they can't be recalled once they leave the building.
  • Lock the office computer when you step away.

If your church stores data in an online system, ask how it's protected and who at the company can access it.

Keep it current

An outdated directory causes small frustrations, like returned mail and wrong numbers, and occasionally real hurt, like a letter addressed to someone who has passed away. A light, steady routine helps.

  • As you hear it: update records when someone mentions a move, a new baby, a marriage or a loss.
  • Monthly: check returned mail and bounced emails and follow up.
  • Once a year: invite every household to review its own information. Many churches do this in the fall or at the start of the year.
  • Every so often: gently mark households who haven't attended in a long time as inactive rather than deleting them, so their history is kept but they aren't listed publicly.

Handle changes with care. When a death, divorce or other painful change occurs, update records quietly and promptly so no one is hurt by an automated message or an outdated listing.

When something goes wrong

If a directory, list or login is lost or exposed, act quickly: change passwords, remove access and tell your pastor and leadership. Depending on what was exposed and where your church is located, there may be legal obligations, so check with your attorney about next steps.
